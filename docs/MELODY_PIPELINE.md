# Melody pipeline diagnosis and repair — 2026-11-21

## Weak-fundamental recovery and limited harmonic backing

The sparse human take also contains stable harmonic families that a single strict YIN trough misses or assigns to an overtone. Sparse analyses now receive a separate 128 ms spectral check: at least three distinct, coherent harmonics must explain at least 90% of retained narrow-peak power, beat competing candidates, and persist for at least 120 ms. Central 40 ms harmonic projections and 10 ms energy checks prevent a long FFT window from turning brief bursts or silence into longer evidence. Broadband noise, conflicting partials and unsupported gaps remain rejected. Confirmed families supplement missing/aliased YIN frames; the recording waveform is never filtered or altered by this analysis.

This is positive evidence for **limited** harmony, not a relaxed full-transcription threshold. The existing 55% full-coverage requirement remains unchanged. At least three validated anchors, two pitch classes and 0.75 seconds of combined support can authorize `status:partial`. The arranger uses those anchors to guide chords and confines pitched backing to their measured source-clock regions. Empty ending layers remain unavailable with an explanation. A partial take does not establish a complete accurate note sequence, and detected MIDI remains approximate.

The user's current 7.62-second take has nine such anchors totalling 1.84 seconds. Reanalysis recovers 16 estimated notes and about 32.7% voiced duration. Guitar, piano, violin and cello now receive real source-supported backing events; the complete original hum stays the lead. Two older human takes still provide insufficient independent evidence and remain blocked from guessed pitched backing. They are not silently substituted or declared repaired.

## Production Director and independent layers

`POST /api/production-director` turns instructions and the current accepted/draft blueprint into a validated backing proposal. The server optionally uses OpenAI structured outputs; without configuration it returns explicitly labelled local rules. The blueprint contains only supported backing instruments, roles, gains, intensity, section entries and bounded pattern variants. It has no editable source audio, notes, contours or lead selection. The first-take direction and the studio expose the same draft/review/Apply flow.

When present, the blueprint replaces legacy backing, while `buildArrangement` retains the same protected lead contract. Guitar, piano, violin, cello, synth, bass and drums generate independent MIDI-style events; each event carries its layer ID. Final-quarter entry is a real source-clock boundary. Regenerating one layer changes its bounded variant without altering other layer event sequences. Gain changes route through separate backing buses. Guitar, violin and cello have distinct synthesized voices; these are approximations, not sampled instrument recordings or a commercial mastering service.

The blueprint persists inside validated project settings in both browser storage and SQLite. Detected-melody MIDI export preserves estimated note pitches and timing without switching the recorded lead. MIDI cannot contain the voice waveform or the full continuous pitch contour. Low-evidence real recordings still suppress guessed pitched layers and show the reason; new controls do not override that gate.

## Actual human-take correction

The user's real 7.62-second take exposed a limit that passing synthetic tests did not establish: the current note extractor found only about 27% voiced evidence, with fragments and register aliases. Exact note copying consequently preserved the extractor's mistakes. The user confirmed that the original recording sounded right and explicitly chose to retain the actual hum as the lead.

New recordings/imports therefore select `leadSource:recording`. The source-bound normalized WAV, independently checked again by the renderer, plays at rate 1 for its complete duration. There is no synthesized melody doubling. Only bounded constant gain, native resampling and up to 3 ms outer-edge fades affect it. Insufficient pitch evidence suppresses guessed chords/bass while keeping requested unpitched rhythm. Instrumental mode remains explicit and approximate; producer suggestions cannot silently change the lead source. Invalid imports retain the prior take, and missing source audio fails instead of substituting notes.

Native rendering of this actual take passed in both pop styles: rounded zero-lag source-waveform correlation 1.000000, residual RMS 0.000016 after fitted fixed gain, complete 7.62-second duration, no clipping, and lead/backing RMS ratios about 2.27 and 3.29. These measurements concern the normalized mono source, not byte identity with stereo WebM or verified human transcription. See [full repair evidence](verification/actual-hum-repair-results.json). Source audio is session-only; reopening requires reattachment or a previously exported WAV.

The old pipeline never passed recorded audio to music generation. It kept the encoded Blob for playback, discarded it from arranging, and sent live rAF/YIN note guesses as JSON. The optional OpenAI producer proposed six sound controls; it was not an audio generator. Continuous-PCM reproduction exposed short-note loss, merged rearticulations and delayed boundaries. Integer MIDI grouping discarded contour. Tempo changes retimed the lead, and long string releases plus shared echo obscured short notes and rests.

## Instrumental reconstruction path

1. Decode the completed recorded file using native Web Audio. Bound PCM at the actual capture duration, retaining the original Blob for playback. Native decoding/resampling avoids premature linear-decimation aliasing.
2. Upload actual PCM WAV to `POST /api/melody/analyze`, with `Content-Type: audio/wav`. Limits are 16 MB and 60 seconds. The SHA-256 receipt identifies exactly the WAV bytes received by the local server, not the original encoded WebM/MP4 container.
3. Analyze complete PCM in a worker, independent of animation frames: bandlimited 16 kHz resampling, centered 64 ms YIN windows every 10 ms, separate energy-based rests, note sequence/contour smoothing, rhythm and voiced-coverage reporting. This remains monophonic pitch estimation, not verified human transcription.
4. Retain at most five source takes for 30 minutes in local memory. A conditioning ID binds each analyzed file to its exact extracted pitch, onset, duration and contour sequence. Substituted notes are rejected. An explicit note edit drops this original-source binding and transposes its contour with the edited pitch.
5. `POST /api/arrange` accepts `{notes, settings, conditioningId?}`. The default `preserveMelodyTiming:true` copies the lead unchanged; backing BPM, chords, bass, percussion and timbre can vary independently. `assertMelodyPreserved` rejects missing, changed, reordered, retimed or contour-altered lead notes. `melodyProof` proves this symbolic contract, not correct transcription of unannotated audio. Explicit `preserveMelodyTiming:false` retains the earlier tempo-transform behavior.
6. Playback/WAV synthesis follows continuous pitch automation on a separate dry lead bus. Fast attack and inside-gate release preserve short gates and rests. Backing retains its own effects at lower gain. MIDI export preserves discrete pitches and gates; it does not encode continuous pitch bends.

Empty, invalid or failed audio never falls back to a random song or the live preview notes. The original take remains available. Automatic/manual finish callbacks for the same take share one analysis promise. Note contours survive HTTP and project save/reopen; JSON transport is bounded at 512 KiB.

The subsequent full recheck also corrected high-note subharmonics, capture-cutoff channel selection, strict WAV chunk boundaries, MIDI lead/backing channel collisions, restricted instrument requests and persistence validation. Expired conditioning is disposed on schedule; an active session can renew it once from the retained original, accepting the new source only when its received-audio SHA and complete extracted melody match. Edits outside the supported contour range leave the previous melody intact. The optional model bounds concurrency and cancels pending work on shutdown. That earlier [full recheck](verification/full-recheck-results.json) recorded 272 Node, 8 Python and 45 browser results; current MusicGen verification is separate below.

## Melody-conditioned neural alternative

[Meta MusicGen Melody](https://github.com/facebookresearch/audiocraft/blob/main/docs/MUSICGEN.md) accepts waveform-derived chroma conditioning. The local CPU implementation now runs the same official `facebook/musicgen-melody` checkpoint through Transformers 4.57.3, with dynamic int8 decoder weights and decoder SDPA. Approximately 6.23 GB of model weights are cached locally. See [local setup and licensing](MUSICGEN_LOCAL.md), [Meta's model card](https://huggingface.co/facebook/musicgen-melody) and the [full verification report](MUSICGEN_VERIFICATION.md). No paid inference or remote recording upload is used.

The initial Transformers empty-cache path discarded the decoder conditioning prefix. Its 483.657-second trial was **rejected**, despite returning a WAV. The compatibility correction is tested against the installed official preparation method. Runtime hooks now fail closed unless the text encoder receives the original prompt IDs, the audio projection receives the original positive-batch chroma features, and the first decoder prefix contains the expected projected audio frames followed by nonzero text conditioning. Cached later decoder calls may omit that prefix. Reports retain plain counts, flags and a feature hash, without tensors.

Genuine conditioned inference subsequently produced a 4.94-second WAV from a five-second request using the real 7.62-second take, in 305.666 Node-job seconds. Native playback reached the end of both original and generated recordings, and the generated WAV was downloaded. Five synthetic source/style cases also completed with all three runtime checks true; four-second requests produced 3.94-second output, taking 150.844–299.953 provider-model seconds. These measurements exclude model download from the per-case provider timings and do not support a 20–40 second CPU target.

`POST /api/generation/jobs` accepts the original `conditioningId`, stable `requestId`, creative `text`, settings and requested duration. The local [production compiler](PRODUCER_KNOWLEDGE.md) produces a bounded music description with transparent technique references. GET/DELETE `/api/generation/jobs/:id` provides progress or cancellation; `/api/generation/jobs/:id/audio` serves only successful validated WAV output. Lost acknowledgements reuse the same request identity. One active inference, a bounded queue, retention limits, cancellation and supervised worker deadlines constrain resources. The browser keeps the prior preview on failure, supports reconnecting to the same request and cleans up replaced audio URLs.

`GET /api/melody/model/status` distinguishes configuration, reachability, loading and readiness. Progress is indeterminate until genuine model token counts exist. Output PCM, model identity, duration and source SHA/byte receipts are validated, and `X-Muse-Exact-Melody-Verified:false` remains explicit. Missing models, invalid audio, errors and timeouts fail without text-only or unconditional substitute audio. The adapter accepts literal loopback HTTP destinations only and rejects redirects. Neural previews remain separate from the protected original lead: chroma conditioning does not enforce exact note, register or onset preservation, and basic RMS/peak normalization is not professional mastering.

All six exact pitch sequences passed offline tests. Worst measured extraction onset error was 20 ms, offset 10 ms, duration 30 ms.

The observed run passed all six checks: zero pitch sequence errors, maximum rendered lead onset/offset difference 10 ms, repeated-note gap 40 ms, and retained vibrato. Backing tempo changes produced byte-identical lead WAVs and different full mixes. Full polyphonic mix detections were reported rather than used to claim exact transcription; audible lead prominence remains a listening check.

Two existing private human takes were processed locally without publishing audio. The 8.76-second take yielded 19 notes with 26.48% voiced coverage; the 9.30-second take yielded six notes with 9.03% coverage and a limited-evidence warning. More detections do not establish correct human transcription. With these two takes plus the six generated fixtures, 40 arrangements across five styles/tempos had zero symbolic lead pitch/timing/contour changes. Human transcription ground truth remains unverified; the later actual MusicGen runs verify the conditioning path and output, not exact neural melody preservation.
